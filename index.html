01 — Problem

Ships are stuck
in the past.

118,000+

Ships. Scattered information.

Ships worldwide, as cited in our project research. Navigating crowded or hazardous waters puts existing tools under pressure.

A

Information overload

Captains juggle paper charts, radar and other instruments, increasing mental strain and reducing situational awareness.

B

Disconnected data

Separate sources make it difficult to build a single, reliable picture of the surroundings.

C

Slow response

In bad weather or heavy traffic, scattered information makes quick decisions harder.

The problem we defined
Maritime navigation still relies heavily on traditional methods, limiting situational awareness and decision-making in complex or hazardous environments.

03 — Platform & technical build

Hands-free,
on the bridge.

We chose HoloLens 2 to keep captains’ and officers’ hands free on the ship bridge. The concept supports three core tasks:

AR dashboards
Ship data and surroundings.
Remote help desk
On-shore support for troubleshooting.
Smart alerts
Audio and proposed touch feedback for urgent information.
Markerless AR

Computer vision interprets the environment. GPS places information geographically. Sensor fusion tracks position and orientation without predefined markers.

04 — Concept & ideation

From sketches
to a spatial system.

We grouped features into heading and position, planning and awareness, and safety and vision. Sketches and storyboards explored how these would sit within the bridge environment.

05 — Interaction design

Eyes on
the sea.

We based the interface on Microsoft Fluent 2 and Segoe typography for consistency and readability in mixed reality.

Designed for comfort

Critical information sits in central vision; peripheral cues draw attention when needed. Interactive elements stay at eye level, within arm’s reach.

Input / 01

Speak to navigate.

Open screens without searching manually.

“Hey Cortana, show me the tidal map”
Input / 02

Touch to explore.

Tap objects, zoom and pan with intuitive gestures.

Output / 01

See it in context.

Overlay depth, hazards, warnings and nearby vessels onto the real world.

Output / 02

Hear the direction.

Spatial audio points towards danger; gesture sounds confirm an action.

Colour as a signal

Green ClearYellow CautionRed Critical
Fitts’s law

Vary target size to suit distance, movement and the interaction.

Miller’s law

Limit the information shown to support comprehension and manage cognitive load.

Jakob’s law

Keep vessel interface conventions familiar to reduce confusion.

08 — Reflection

What it would
take at sea.

Limits to resolve

  • Ruggedness. The system must withstand water, wind and rough conditions.
  • Positioning accuracy. Overlays must remain reliable when GPS signals weaken.

Next steps from the project

  • Real-time maintenance and repair overlays.
  • Shared information between crew and workstations.
  • Navigation and training on larger displays.
  • Interactive tutorials inside the AR environment.
